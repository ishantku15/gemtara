Ruby: The King of Gemstones — Everything You Need to Know

July 15, 2026 7 min read

Throughout history, the ruby has reigned supreme as the undisputed king of gemstones. The ancient Sanskrit word for ruby, 'ratnaraj', literally translates to 'king of precious stones'. Its fiery allure has captured the hearts of royalty, warriors, and collectors for centuries, making it one of the most sought-after and valuable colored gemstones in the world.

The Geology of the King: Corundum and Chromium

Rubies belong to the corundum mineral family, which is composed of aluminum oxide. In its purest form, corundum is completely colorless. However, nature adds a touch of magic in the form of trace elements. When chromium is present in the crystal lattice of corundum during its formation, it imparts a vibrant red hue—transforming ordinary corundum into the magnificent ruby.

Interestingly, if other trace elements like iron or titanium are present, the corundum becomes a sapphire. This makes rubies and sapphires essentially siblings, sharing identical physical and optical properties, save for their color.

Mohs Hardness: Built to Last

One of the reasons rubies are so highly prized is their incredible durability. On the Mohs scale of mineral hardness, rubies score an impressive 9. This means they are second only to diamonds in terms of resistance to scratching. This exceptional hardness makes rubies ideal for everyday wear, particularly in engagement rings.

The Pinnacle of Color: Pigeon Blood Red

When it comes to ruby grading, color is the absolute king. The most valuable rubies exhibit a pure, vibrant red with a subtle hint of blue, often referred to as "pigeon blood" red. This term historically originated in Myanmar (formerly Burma), which has long been the source of the world's finest rubies.

"The fire of a fine Burmese ruby is unlike anything else on earth—it glows from within with an unquenchable flame."

Common Treatments

  • Heat Treatment: A highly common and generally accepted practice. Heating rough rubies can dissolve inclusions and improve the overall color and clarity.
  • Fracture Filling: Lower-quality rubies are often filled with lead glass to improve their apparent clarity. These stones are much less valuable and require special care.

Famous Rubies

The Sunrise Ruby is a 25.59-carat Burmese pigeon blood ruby that sold for a record-breaking $30 million. The Liberty Bell Ruby, the largest mined ruby, was carved into the shape of the Liberty Bell but was tragically stolen in 2011.

Buying and Care Tips

Always ask for a gemological report from a reputable lab (like the GIA) when purchasing a ruby, as it will disclose any treatments. To clean your ruby, simply use warm soapy water and a soft brush.
