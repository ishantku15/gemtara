Moonstone: Mystical Properties & the Glow of Adularescence

Aug 12, 2026 8 min read

Few gemstones capture the imagination quite like the moonstone. With its ethereal glow that seems to billow across the surface like moonlight on water, this captivating gem has been shrouded in myth and magic for centuries. It is the gem of intuition, feminine energy, and deep emotional balance. As one of the modern birthstones for June, moonstone continues to enchant jewelry designers, collectors, and spiritual seekers alike.

The Feldspar Family and the Magic of Adularescence

To understand the magic of moonstone, one must look at its unique geological formation. Moonstone belongs to the feldspar group, the most abundant mineral group in the Earth's crust. Specifically, classical moonstone is composed of two types of feldspar: orthoclase and albite.

During the formation of the crystal deep within the earth, these two minerals intermingle. As the mineral cools, they separate into alternating, microscopic, wafer-like layers. When light enters the stone, it strikes these thin, alternating layers and scatters in many directions. This optical phenomenon is known as adularescence.

The term adularescence comes from "Adularia," an old name for an early mining site for moonstone in the Adula Mountains of Switzerland. The effect is best described as a milky, bluish, or silvery light that appears to float just below the surface of the gem, moving as the stone is turned.

Varieties of Moonstone: From Rainbows to Stars

While the classic silvery-white moonstone is the most recognized, the gem comes in several fascinating varieties:

  • Sri Lankan Blue Sheen: Highly prized and increasingly rare, these stones from Sri Lanka display a nearly transparent body color with a striking, vivid blue adularescence that sweeps across the surface.
  • Indian Moonstone: Typically found in earthy shades of peach, orange, brown, and green. These stones often show a white adularescent sheen rather than blue.
  • Star Moonstone: Occasionally, the inclusions within the feldspar are aligned perfectly to create a star effect (asterism) or a cat's eye effect (chatoyancy) when the stone is cut en cabochon.
  • Rainbow Moonstone (The Labradorite Exception): It is a crucial gemological fact that the popular "rainbow moonstone"—known for its flashes of blue, green, yellow, and pink—is technically not a traditional orthoclase moonstone at all. It is actually a transparent variety of labradorite (another type of feldspar) that exhibits labradorescence. However, the trade name "rainbow moonstone" is universally accepted in the market.

Mystical and Crystal Healing Properties

Throughout history, moonstone has been intimately tied to the lunar cycle, intuition, and divine feminine energy. In ancient Rome, it was believed that moonstone was formed from drops of actual moonlight, and in India, it has always been considered a sacred stone.

"Moonstone is a stone of inner growth and strength. It soothes emotional instability and stress, and stabilizes the emotions, providing calmness. It enhances intuition, promotes inspiration, success, and good fortune in love and business matters."

In the practice of crystal healing, moonstone is deeply connected to the Crown Chakra and the Third Eye Chakra, making it a powerful tool for meditation and spiritual insight. It is often referred to as a "stone of new beginnings," encouraging the wearer to embrace the cycles of change, much like the waxing and waning of the moon.

It is heavily utilized to balance female energies, alleviate menstrual tension, and support emotional healing. For those who feel disconnected from their intuitive self, meditating with moonstone is said to unlock inner wisdom and enhance psychic abilities.

Care and Handling of Moonstone

While moonstone is stunning, it requires careful handling. With a hardness of 6 to 6.5 on the Mohs scale, it is significantly softer than quartz, sapphire, or diamond. More importantly, moonstone possesses perfect cleavage in two directions. This means that a sharp blow from the wrong angle can cause the stone to split or break entirely.

Because of its fragility, moonstone is best suited for pendants, earrings, and brooches rather than rings or bracelets intended for daily wear. If set in a ring, a protective setting, such as a bezel setting, is highly recommended to shield the edges of the stone.

Cleaning: Never use ultrasonic or steam cleaners on moonstone. The intense vibrations and heat can cause the stone to fracture along its cleavage planes. Clean your moonstone gently using only warm water, a very mild soap, and a soft cloth. Store it away from harder gemstones to prevent scratches.

The glowing allure of moonstone remains timeless. Whether viewed through the lens of mineralogical fascination or mystical reverence, this phenomenal gem continues to shine brightly as a talisman of the night sky.
