Citrine: The Merchant's Stone — Guide & Benefits

Aug 11, 2026 8 min read

Radiating with the warmth and vitality of the sun, citrine is a gemstone that instantly uplifts the spirit. Known affectionately as the "Merchant's Stone" for its long-standing association with wealth and prosperity, this golden-yellow variety of quartz has been adored for millennia. Whether you are drawn to its cheerful aesthetics, its powerful crystal healing properties, or its historical significance, citrine offers a wealth of benefits and fascinating characteristics to explore.

The Quartz Family and the Heat Treatment Controversy

Citrine is a macrocrystalline variety of quartz, sharing the same fundamental chemical composition (silicon dioxide) as amethyst, rock crystal, and rose quartz. The distinct yellow to brownish-orange hues of natural citrine are caused by traces of iron in the quartz crystal structure, combined with natural irradiation within the earth over millions of years.

The Rarity of Natural Citrine

It may surprise many gem enthusiasts to learn that natural citrine is exceedingly rare in nature. The vast majority of the "citrine" available on the market today is actually amethyst or smoky quartz that has been subjected to high-temperature heat treatment.

When purple amethyst is heated to temperatures between 470°C and 560°C, its color centers are altered, transforming the stone into a bright yellow or orange hue. This practice is entirely stable and widely accepted in the gem trade, but it has led to a split among crystal purists and healers, some of whom argue that only natural citrine carries the true energetic frequency of the stone.

Identifying Natural vs. Heat-Treated Citrine

Distinguishing between the two can often be done with a trained eye:

  • Color: Heat-treated citrine typically displays an intense, slightly unnatural orange, reddish-orange, or amber color, often with a white base (where the amethyst crystal matrix was). Natural citrine is usually a much paler, subtle, earthy yellow or champagne color, with consistent coloration throughout the crystal.
  • Structure: Heat-treated stones often retain the "geode" or "cluster" shape characteristic of amethyst. Natural citrine rarely forms in geodes and is more commonly found in single, terminated points or massive formations.
  • Dichroism: Natural citrine exhibits weak dichroism (showing different colors when viewed from different angles), whereas heat-treated stones typically do not.

Madeira Citrine

The most prized and expensive variety of citrine is known as Madeira citrine. Named after the fortified wine from the Portuguese Madeira Islands, this variety boasts a deep, rich brownish-red to orange color. While some natural Madeira citrine exists, most on the market is achieved through the careful heating of high-quality amethyst.

The association of citrine with financial success extends deeply into Feng Shui practices. To harness its wealth-attracting energies, practitioners recommend strategic placement of citrine within the home or office.

Placing a large citrine crystal cluster, or even a small tumbled stone, in this area is believed to activate financial flow and encourage business success. Additionally, keeping a small piece of citrine in your cash register, wallet, or financial document drawer is a common practice among merchants and business owners.

Caring for Your Citrine

With a Mohs hardness of 7, citrine is highly suitable for all types of jewelry, including rings designed for daily wear. It is resistant to scratching but can still be damaged by sharp blows or extreme temperature changes.

The most crucial aspect of caring for citrine is light protection. Prolonged exposure to direct, intense sunlight can cause the color of both natural and heat-treated citrine to fade over time. When you are not wearing your citrine jewelry, store it in a dark, cool place.

Cleaning is straightforward: use warm, soapy water and a soft brush. Ultrasonic cleaners are generally safe, but steam cleaning is not recommended due to the risk of thermal shock.

Whether you appreciate it for its autumnal beauty, its fascinating geological origins, or its powerful metaphysical vibrations, citrine is a gemstone that brings light, energy, and joy to anyone who encounters it.
